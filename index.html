About Me

I recently completed an MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics at CY Cergy Paris Université and ENSEA, following a research internship at the ETIS Laboratory.

My work sits where computer vision meets computational neuroscience: taking a raw visual signal and asking whether a biologically plausible learning rule can develop the kind of representation a brain actually uses. My thesis built an unsupervised model of entorhinal speed cells driven purely by monocular optical flow, and showed that it reproduces both the classical speed tuning and the more recent finding that this tuning rescales with the geometry of the surroundings.

I am currently looking for PhD positions and research roles in computer vision, self-supervised and multimodal representation learning, trustworthy machine learning, and bio-inspired autonomous systems.

Professional Experience

April 2026 - September 2026

Research Internship, Optical Flow and Bio-inspired Speed Cells

ETIS Laboratory, CY Cergy Paris Université. Supervisors: Prof. Nicolas Cuperlier and Mariem Thabet

Built a CARLA-based monocular visual ego-speed estimation pipeline using Shi-Tomasi feature detection, Lucas-Kanade optical flow and ground-plane projection, reaching 2.9 to 12.2 per cent relative error across three simulated environments.

Designed an unsupervised Hebbian-Oja artificial speed-cell population of 50 units that reproduces seven biological speed-cell properties directly from optical flow, with no speed labels used during training.

Showed that a geometry-cancelled baseline population is context invariant, matching Kropff (2015), while a multiplicative geometry-injection model becomes sensitive to enclosure, matching Munn (2020), confirmed with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.

Validated robustness across 6 random seeds and 3 population sizes, and generalisation to held-out frames and an unseen town.
